Sandbox streaming validation and Windows reproduction

Normal frames call World.streamAsync with a 0.5 ms owner deadline. The existing
DetailedMeshScheduler receives the remainder of a 1 ms preparation allowance.
Lighting receives the shared allowance minus measured terrain, mesh and focus
work: 2 ms with current nearby lighting and no pending GI/sky work, 4 ms for
missing coverage or pending useful GI/sky preparation or upload. Initial lighting can use 8 ms for at
most 64 updates. This does not meet the proposed 2 ms loading-tail target.

These are cooperative deadlines. A GL call, JVM compilation or safepoint can
overrun a unit. Timings and bytes are exported; the limits are not hard real-time
guarantees. GPU lanes alternate first service and reuse idle time. They share
4 MiB per normal frame, 8 MiB initially. GI transfers use at most 1 MiB per unit;
independent sky uses 256 KiB. Sixteen fenced 1 MiB staging buffers are polled
without waiting. Two GPU store generations per role are prepared at startup.
Normal arrivals refill them without allocating whole stores. Exceptional fine
geometry retains the supported capacity-growth fallback. GPU work and cleanup
stay on the context thread.

A single terrain worker owns its Terrain and Geography caches. It also handles
bounded sky tile and atlas work, alternating those tasks with terrain jobs. Its
queue retains at most eight queued/running/completed chunks, including obsolete
running work. The owner can retain one more result for incremental edit replay.
Workers contain detached roots, positions, immutable settings and worker-owned
terrain. They cannot reach live World maps, authoritative edits or GL objects.
The existing mesh scheduler is reused; there is no second meshing system.

World identity, position epochs, edit/model revisions and neighbor dependencies
fence generation and mesh results. Current edits replay on the owner before
attachment. Duplicate requests coalesce. Teleport/unload/shutdown cancel obsolete
work. Generated terrain carries seed/version/grid/position provenance: equivalent
residency changes do not invalidate lighting, but edits, custom geometry, model
changes and a different or closed world do. Partial columns retain distant-parent
coverage. Missing collision terrain remains solid.

Startup keeps drawing and polling a cancellable loading screen while nearby
terrain and the first current GI/sky field become usable. It does not render an
unlit world merely to advance upload ticks. Normal gameplay, respawn and teleport
use the bounded frame path. A fresh or invalidated area gets the actual camera
position first. Moving GI forecasts advance at most 32 m from current usable
coverage. Reversal drops work moving away from the player. A stationary camera
keeps a current covering field. Equivalent results at the same origin and with
unchanged dependencies reuse GPU data; its true capture age is retained.

Sky visibility caches immutable 16x16 tiles. Capture checks deadlines between
roots and edited cells. Completed inputs dispatch without waiting for another
partial tile. Packing preserves progress under continuous arrivals, uses a
capture-start bound, and leaves later additions for the next merge. Sky retains
at most four pending states: worker jobs, partial capture and dirty/input work.
Packed data stays within 8,000,000 ints. CPU sampling remains an independent
binary-search reference.

Worker sky packing clips footprints to integer columns. The highest full-column
opaque ceiling makes all lower occluders redundant for vertical sky rays. Only
those lower segments are removed. Transparent layers, fractional gaps and tiny
model footprints above it remain exact. An optional GPU directory covers at most
512 integer X rows: three ints per row, at most 6 KiB within the existing cap.
Contiguous Z rows map directly to unchanged headers; sparse rows and wider worlds
keep binary search. Merges strip input directory tails before appending one new
index. No sampler, texture format or visual setting changes for this index.

GI workers produce exact half-float transport mip data. The normal prefix path
stores fine roots in the existing integer buffer. A field with no refined cells
omits its zero-root table and uses uniform lookup mode2. Refined cells, including
negative packed uniform-color roots, keep the full prefix path. The proof is
saved before bake scratch is cleared. Legacy 3D roots remain supported. Accepted
old resources remain active until replacements are ready; all buffers and fences
are released on their owning thread.

No graphics defaults, draw distance, quality or horizon settings are reduced.
The test preset requests Balanced 640x480. On this assigned display it falls back
to an actual 1280x720 framebuffer; each report records actual dimensions. Fixed
route settings are seed748291,10:00, detail112, horizon2048, render scale1, dynamic
resolution off and swap interval0. Each run uses a fresh synthetic profile. The
fixture requires the exact same810 initial chunks, finishes their meshes, then
warms graphics. Fresh travel starts at x128 and reaches x236, outside preload.
Resident, cold entry, walking, flight, reversal, teleport and catch-up are separate
segments. Startup and warm-up durations are recorded separately.

Windows owner reproduction:
1. Install/build the controller's exact feature revision through the existing
   client/launcher pipeline. Keep the same JVM, GPU/CPU, resolution and settings
   for baseline and feature. Use separate synthetic profiles/saves.
2. Select seed748291,10:00, Balanced, detail112, horizon2048, render scale1 and
   dynamic resolution off. Record actual settings and framebuffer resolution.
3. Keep F10 off. Use F9 CSV/JSON capture after consistent warm-up. Walk into fresh
   terrain, hold flight+sprint for several minutes, reverse, teleport/respawn,
   then revisit the route. Repeat three fresh-save trials for each revision.
4. Compare frame/stage p50/p95/p99, worst hitches,1% lows and >33/50/100 ms counts.
   Include generation, owner attachment/edit replay, scheduling/snapshot/upload,
   disposal, lighting, shadow/probe, network/GC, queues/age, bytes and memory.
   Separate startup, cold, resident, revisit and transition samples.
5. During load, place/break whole and fractional blocks, RGB LEDs, glass, water
   and tiny models. Have a second synthetic multiplayer client edit a border.
   Unload/reload; verify edits, occlusion, closed seams, collision and old coverage.
6. Switch seed/world and reconnect. Repeat travel and shutdown. No old-world
   results or lost edits may appear. Check loading UI Escape cancellation.
7. Record a short representative F10 run separately. Stop F10 and compare its
   overhead with an unrecorded run. Keep the MP4 below6 MB and exclude private data.
