Change and visual comparison
The original grip was upright but the forearm dominated the first-person image, the exposed blade was short, and every category had the same tilt/facing. World attack jumped directly from idle to shoulder height; first person had no upward wind-up.
The existing HeldToolPose helper now keeps the same lower-handle anchor (8,4,8)/16 while choosing modest shaft tilt and head-facing yaw by category, shared between wooden/stone variants and world/first-person rendering. First-person display scale is .86 instead of .62; the fist and tool move inward, lower and farther from the lens as one transform. The comparison shows more exposed handle/blade relative to the forearm and a clear diagonal silhouette. Low-FOV clipping found during tuning was fixed. At high FOV, the lower sleeve now extends below the screen rather than exposing its base.
Attack joins idle, rises to shoulder height over the first .18 of normalized progress, strikes down to .72, then recovers. Smoothstep joins avoid the previous first-frame snap. First-person hand lifts and then drops; placement retains quarter-strength motion. Anchor contact, positive scale, original meshes/textures, movement logic and network protocol are retained. No per-frame mesh rebuilding was added.
